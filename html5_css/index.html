Fender Precision Bass

The Fender Precision Bass, a.k.a P Bass, was introduced in the early 1950s and become one of the most popular electric bass guitars. It's known for its deep, warm, and powerful sound that really stands out in a rock music. I like playing my P Bass when I want a heavier, grungier tone that fills out the sound of the band. I especially like using it for songs like "Only Happy When it Rains" by Garbage, and "Zombie" by the The Cranberries. The deeper tone of the P Bass feels especially good on songs like these where I want the bass to have some weight behind it. It also has a thicker neck and heavier feel than some of the other basses I play, which gives it a very solid feel in my hands. For me, the P Bass is a great choice when a song calls for the deep, gritty rock sound.

Fender Jazz Bass

The Fender Jazz Bass was introduced in 1960 as another option to the Precision Bass. It has a brighter and more defined sound, along with a slimmer neck that can make it easier to move around the fretboard. I used to play a Fender Jazz Bass and liked it's versatility and the way the individual notes came through clearly. It works well for songs in our set list where I wanted a cleaner bass tone with a little more punch. I no longer play my Fender Jazz Bass because I prefer my Schecter, which gives me the Jazz Bass feel and sound that I like while being more comfortable for me to play. My Schecter has become one of my main basses and is the one reach for when I want that style of sound. You can learn more about why I prefer it on my Schecter page
